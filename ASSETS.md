# Asset provenance

All game assets were produced for this project. Art direction uses original warm, painterly anime JRPG imagery; combat references posture and timed-deflection mechanics.

## Current Mio: user-provided low-poly Tripo model — 2026-10-16

The user sent a lower-polygon Mio from Tripo Studio to a live Blender session over Tripo DCC Bridge. It arrived as "3d fantasy character model": one 47,383-triangle mesh, a 65-bone Mixamo rig, no clips, and packed 4096×4096 base-color, normal, roughness and metallic maps.

- Bridge save: `assets/source/tripo-bridge-20261016-mio.blend` (SHA-256 `3cbb7b35…`), saved unchanged with `save_as_mainfile(copy=True)`.
- Repaired source: `assets/source/mio-lowpoly-fixed.blend` (SHA-256 `5ab0831f…`), Mio alone with the default cube/camera/light.
  - Base color: the bake printed skin and white onto the bangs, side locks and the strands behind the ears. Off-colour texels on those strands were refilled from nearby hair texels, with eyes, ears, shirt collar and nape masked out. The JPEG was re-saved once with its original quantization tables.
  - Normal map: the bake had steep, wrong normals around the eyes, lapels, cuffs and collar. All head texels and every steep normal elsewhere were flattened; the mild cloth detail remains.
  - Three isolated triangles left by the reduction were deleted, leaving 47,380.
  - Repair scripts and before/after renders: `artifacts/mio-lowpoly-hairfix/`.
- Face repair: `assets/source/mio-lowpoly-fixed-face.blend` (SHA-256 `da45ca7d…`) is the repaired source with one more base-color pass. Dark strokes from the bake on her cheeks, beside her left eye and on the nose bridge were traced on close-up renders, mapped to texels by ray casting, and refilled from the surrounding skin. Eyes, brows and the eyeliner wings were left alone.
- Clips: the user then rigged the same model in Tripo Studio and made its clips there. They arrived over DCC Bridge as "3d fantasy character model.002" with fifteen actions; the unchanged save is `assets/source/tripo-bridge-20261016-mio-anim.blend` (SHA-256 `8a4e5eea…`). Its mesh and UVs equal the first send's vertex for vertex.
  - Tripo presets: idle, walk, run, jump.
  - Generated from the text prompts in `artifacts/mio-tripo-upload/mio-tripo-animation-guide.md`: attack1–3, guard, dodge, skill, stagger and death. The guide's attack1 prompt was replaced by a stronger "steps forward and swings" prompt after review.
  - Unused: the slash and standing_relax presets and a first, weak attack1.
- Build: `tools/import_tripo_mio.py` keeps Tripo's 65-bone Mixamo rig and weights, with the `mixamorig:` prefix removed. Each clip is cut to its motion and retimed to a game length; the fastest swing of each attack sits within two frames of its hit. Hips are held over the collision body. On top of Tripo's motion:
  - The three attacks open from and settle into the attack1 prompt's ready stance over 3 and 4 frames, so the combo chains through one pose.
  - The jump keeps the first of the preset's repeated hops with its vertical travel removed, then adds an authored crouch on the ground frames and a knee tuck in the air, because the preset hops with straight legs.
  - The dodge uses the whole roll (crouch, roll, rise) in 15 frames.
  - The right fingers close around the hilt in every clip, with the thumb from the slash preset's tightest fist; the generated clips left them straight.
  - Guard plays its held block forward and back as a loop.
- The textures come from the face-repaired source; corner normals are recalculated. Tripo's roughness and metallic maps are not used; the material keeps the previous Mio's roughness 0.9 and metallic 0. The original sword is fitted to `RightHand`. Audit: `tools/check_mio_export.py`.
- Runtime: `assets/models/mio.glb`, 65 bones, 12 clips, 47,980 triangles including the sword, 1.84 m tall, 8.4 MB. Editable game copy: `assets/source/mio-tripo-game.blend`.
- Textures: `assets/art/mio-lowpoly-basecolor.jpg` and `mio-lowpoly-normal.png`, the repaired source images, embedded byte-for-byte in the GLB.
- Backups: the dense-rig low-poly GLB is `artifacts/mio-tripo-animation/before-mio.glb`; the earlier GLB and game copy are in `artifacts/mio-lowpoly-integration/before/`.
- Limits: coat and hair have no bones of their own. Tripo's run is a jog, so at 7.8 m/s its feet still slide. A slightly paler patch remains where the stroke beside her left eye was.

### Material atlas prompt

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: game material texture atlas for a 3D anime JRPG rural Japanese village. Create one square 2048x2048 texture atlas divided into four perfectly equal square quadrants, edge to edge, no margins, no text, no borders. Top left: seamless richly detailed hand-painted meadow grass, tiny green blades, clover, subtle dark moss, varied sage and olive greens, no large flowers. Top right: seamless warm ochre packed earth footpath, small pebbles, worn granular soil and pale dusty mottling. Bottom left: seamless old warm grey irregular cut stone masonry with fine cracks and soft moss in seams, subtle surface grain. Bottom right: seamless terracotta Japanese ceramic roof tiles, repeating orderly overlapping rows, fine handmade variation, weathering and muted red brown glazed edges. Each quadrant is a flat orthographic PBR base color texture, lighting neutral and evenly diffuse, no perspective, no cast shadows. Painterly detailed game texture finish, attractive restrained natural palette, readable fine organic detail. No objects, logos, labels, watermarks.

Three-quarter front view from slightly above, the whole object centred and filling 75% of a square frame, on a plain flat light grey background; no ground, no shadow, no scenery, no text. A waist-high round ring of rough grey fieldstone with moss in the joints, its top covered by a lid of weathered wooden boards. Semi-realistic painterly hand-painted PBR game asset, weathered and aged, warm natural colours. Soft even diffuse front light, no strong shadows, no rim light, true albedo colours. - **Codex reference prompt** (prepared but not run: Codex was at its usage limit and agy's backend returned errors, so Tripo's own text-to-image made the reference instead): "A single isolated 3D-game prop concept render, used as the input for an image-to-3D model generator: an old Japanese village well (ido) from a rural Edo-period hamlet, seen whole from a three-quarter front view slightly above eye level, centred, filling about 75% of a square frame, on a perfectly plain flat light grey background with no ground, no floor shadow, no scenery and no text. - **Inspection:** renders from four sides and of the maps show no baked-texture errors: no seams, smears, holes or projected background. The ORM metal channel is about 0 and the normal map is mild.
- **Placement:** `World.cs` stands the well at its old spot, `Height(-3.2, 24)`, turned 90° so its posts span east-west like the old well. It is not baked by `Merge()`. Its collision is fitted by hand: a ring cylinder, two post boxes, and a camera-only roof box (`CameraLayer`).
- **Brightness (phase 2b):** Tripo's albedo reads darker than the village atlas, so `World.Model()` gives each Tripo model one copy of its material, shared by its instances, with an albedo tint. The well's is 1.45. The cedar props (benches, lamps, barrels) share the cedar atlas material, now tinted (1.55, 1.5, 1.45) for a lighter, weathered grey-brown, with no new material.
